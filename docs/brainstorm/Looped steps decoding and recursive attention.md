# Loop inside each latent step first

Build idea A first. That means a loop over GPT-2's middle layers inside each of the six latent steps, with four properties: it adds no KV entries, it is initialized so that every loop count reproduces the released checkpoint answer for answer, it is trained with a per-step signal, and it is judged against a ft6 trained with the same objective and a ft12 matched on compute. A is the only candidate that adds serial computation without adding KV entries or positions. Those are the two things this project has shown cost accuracy past the trained depth: near-copy dilution at GPT-2 scale and RoPE answer distance at 1B. Its family also has the best record on this exact task. **PCCoT's state-replacing Jacobi iterations reach 49.48 on GPT-2 GSM8K, and LOTUS's looped latents reach 44.1 against a CODI rerun at 42.0**, while loop retrofits of pretrained ~1B models gain 5–12 GSM8K points. The cautions are just as concrete. Those retrofits used about 50B tokens, **iterating every token in TaH's AlwaysThink baseline cost about 6 points**, and looped models can learn to ignore the loop. So I expect a short fine-tune to gain 0 to +2 points, inside single-seed noise, which is why the plan rests on a paired loop-count sweep within one model and on three seeds. Idea D ranks second. Its decoding diagnostics need no training, cost about one T4-hour and share infrastructure with A, so they are worth running, but as a method D enters a crowded literature, throws away latent content, and must beat gpt2-sft given the same calculator. Idea F ranks third as a stand-alone method. A key-key bias provably cancels duplicate entries where a per-entry gate cannot, but any readout reweighting is capped at holding the 6-step score, and its training-free form belongs to the analysis study. F earns a real job only beside a version of A that writes one KV entry per loop iteration. Either outcome is publishable, because nobody has compared depth inside a sequential latent step with more latent steps at matched compute.

## Every repair so far held the 6-step score and none beat it

The baselines in our harness set a narrow target. On the 500-question GSM8K-Aug validation split, plain **gpt2 gets 2 right (0.4%), gpt2-sft 212 (42.4%), gpt2-codi 206 (41.2%) and gpt2-sim-cot 204** ([leaderboard](../../results/leaderboard.csv); [Stage 1 CODI report](../../results/latent_gate/stage1/codi/report.txt)). On the 1,319-question test set our CODI scores 42.46% against gpt2-sft's 42.30% ([leaderboard](../../results/leaderboard.csv)), and SIM-CoT GPT-2 scores 39.3% against its paper's 42.6% ([held-out results](../LATENT_GATE_HELDOUT_RESULTS.md)). Latent CoT in our harness is therefore at parity with explicit CoT, not above it. Running past the trained depth costs answers: 206/192/173 for CODI and 204/185/170 for SIM-CoT at 6/12/24 steps. Every intervention so far has at most restored the 6-step score.

| Intervention | Result | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Training-free fixes at 24 steps | `keep_first` 205 and `dedup_soft` 201 on CODI; `dedup_soft` 207 on SIM-CoT | Back to the 6-step score, never above it |
| Answer-only fine-tune at 12 steps | CODI ft12 scores 210 at 12 against ft6's 209 at 6 ([report](../../results/latent_gate/stage1/codi/report.txt)); SIM-CoT ft12 scores 207 at 12 against ft6's 215 ([report](../../results/latent_gate/stage1/simcot/report.txt)) | Extra trained steps add nothing |
| Union over depths | Counting a question as solved at any of 6, 12 or 24 steps adds only 9–15 answers ([memory](/Users/stevotrujillo/.claude/projects/-Users-stevotrujillo-Desktop-DSALT/memory/dsa-pivot-latent-eviction.md)) | The extra depth produces almost no new answers |
| `dedup_anchor` at 1B | Holds depth, but no depth beats 6 steps | Holding is not gaining |
| SIM-CoT-1B fine-tune | **Fell from 277 to 246** on validation; training cross-entropy was already flat at 0.04–0.11 ([memory](/Users/stevotrujillo/.claude/projects/-Users-stevotrujillo-Desktop-DSALT/memory/heldout-scale-results.md)) | Answer-only fine-tuning has nothing left to teach at 1B |

The yardstick for all of this is noise. Two SIM-CoT fine-tunes that should match, ft6 and ft6x2, scored 215 and 206, so **one seed moves by about ±10 answers**.

The literature shows that this ceiling is a property of the recipe, not of GPT-2. On GSM8K test, published latent results range from CODI's 43.7 (reruns 42.0 and 40.86) to **PCCoT at 49.48±0.31**, which uses 24 latents and 3 Jacobi iterations; PCCoT's own CODI-style baseline with 12 sequential latents reaches **48.24±1.61** ([PCCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18582v2)). LOTUS reaches 44.1±0.7 ([LOTUS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31779)) and PMPS 44.35 ([PMPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09928)). GPT-2 latent CoT can therefore beat explicit CoT by about five points, but only when more computation is *trained in*, as four parallel passes over 24 latents or as 12 latents trained from base GPT-2 for 40 epochs. Our negative results cover only answer-only fine-tuning of converged 6-step checkpoints. Two constraints follow: a readout-side method cannot add accuracy when the extra entries contain nothing new, and a gain has to come from new trained computation or new outside information. The three candidates differ on exactly these axes.

| | A: inner block loop | D: decode and re-inject | F: recursive attention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source of any new accuracy | Extra serial compute at each latent position | Outside information (a calculator) and fresh discrete input | None on its own; it removes the penalty for redundant entries |
| KV entries / positions added | None / none | About 6–12 entries and positions per injected step | None |
| Closest prior work | LOTUS, PCCoT, TaH, ETD, McLeish et al., Penelope | SpiralThinker, Thinking States, HRPO, SwiReasoning, Chang et al. | ToMe, R-KV, LUCID, Selective Attention, InfiLoop |
| Best comparable reported gain | +1.5 to +2.1 over CODI and SIM-CoT on GPT-2 (LOTUS); +5 to +12 GSM8K in 1B retrofits | +0.9 GSM8K (PCCoT with CoT decoding); +0.1 to +2.8 for training-free hybrids | None on latent CoT; gains on explicit reasoning tokens only |
| What still looks open | A loop inside sequential latent steps; depth vs steps at matched compute | Latent CoT with a calculator; the released decoder used as a verbalizer | A key-key −log multiplicity bias over latent entries past the trained depth |
| Cheapest first test | An evaluation-only probe, then a pilot of about 20 compute units | About 1 T4-hour, evaluation only | Evaluation only, plus minutes of scalar fitting |
| Overlap with the analysis study | None for the trained loop at 6 steps | Decode-and-select and decode-based halting are readout fixes | The training-free version is a stronger soft dedup |
| My estimate, short budget | 0 to +2 points | −3 to +3 points | 0 (hold) |

The last row is my judgement, built from the gains cited below. No paper has measured any of the three on a CODI-family checkpoint.

## Inner loops have the best record and the safest starting point

### Retrofitted loops gain 5–12 GSM8K points, but only with large budgets or selective depth

Adding recurrence to a pretrained LM works on math. McLeish et al. turned TinyLlama into a (prelude, loop, coda) = (4, 8, 4) model and raised GSM8K from **25.6 at one recurrence to 52.0 at 32**, against 46.2 for a static model post-trained on the same data. That took about 50B tokens of math data, and the gains "largely level off between 16 and 32" ([McLeish et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07384v1)). ETD loops a middle block of OLMo-2-1B (layers 7–10 of 16) and goes from **44.05 at k=1 to 56.56 at k=5** after 5×10¹⁰ tokens of mid-training; at matched FLOPs that block beat looping all 16 layers and beat a broad 12-layer loop ([ETD](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07358v1)). Think-at-Hard (TaH, ICML 2026) loops the whole Qwen3 backbone with a depth-aware LoRA active only on extra iterations and reaches **63.6 against 58.2** at 0.6B ([TaH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08577)). T2MLR adds that recurrence over "as little as 20% of the network often beats full-layer recurrence" ([T2MLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15178)). On our exact setting, the two strongest GPT-2 latent methods are also state-replacing iteration schemes. PCCoT reruns the full model over a fixed latent set (49.48), and LOTUS loops the whole model over parallel latent blocks, scoring **44.1 against 42.0 for CODI and 42.6 for SIM-CoT** in the same table and 57.3 on Llama-1B ([LOTUS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31779)). Both overwrite latent states instead of appending them, which is A's defining property.

The cautions are as specific as the gains. TaH's AlwaysThink baseline, which iterates every token twice and then fine-tunes, **lost 5.6 points at 0.6B (58.2 → 52.6) and 6.0 at 1.7B (80.2 → 74.2)**. TaH's own gain comes from iterating only about 7% of tokens, and even then a second pass fixes 8.7% of first-pass predictions while turning 2.1% of correct ones wrong ([TaH](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08577)). Huginn's failed runs either collapsed or "learned to ignore the incoming state," with the same perplexity at 1 and 32 recurrences ([Huginn](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05171)). Reapplying a block on a frozen checkpoint "usually degrades performance," though damped sub-steps gain +2.64 points on MMLU-Pro with Qwen3-4B ([Training-Free Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23872)). Our budget of roughly 10–30M fine-tuning tokens is three to four orders of magnitude below the retrofits that gained 6–12 points. Extrapolation also saturates: LOTUS-3B, trained at R=6, goes from **70.0 to 69.3 at R=7**, and Ouro, trained at T=4, falls on AIME24 from 64.70 to 39.00 at T=8 ([Ouro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)). The realistic target is accuracy that rises with k up to the trained maximum and stays flat beyond it, not "more loops at test time, more GSM8K."

### Nobody has looped inside a sequential latent step

No paper found loops a block of layers inside each *sequential*, CODI-style latent step while writing one KV entry per step; targeted searches turned up only LOTUS, PCCoT and per-token retrofits. Novelty is thin on mechanism, though, and a reviewer will call A "LOTUS or TaH applied to CODI." The nearest relative, Penelope, confines recurrence to "a chosen decoder interval" of a pretrained model for latent reasoning, with a CoT-to-latent curriculum. It was read only at abstract level ([Penelope](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25915)). Its full text, LoopUS's and the LOTUS appendix must be read before any novelty claim.

What is unclaimed is the controlled question. Does depth inside a step beat more appended steps at matched compute, in a model whose appended entries this project has shown dilute the readout? The answer is informative either way. "Trading Depth for Time" found that latent thought tokens (time) overtake loops (depth) as compute grows in its setting ([Trading Depth for Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21605)). Zhang's memory-budget theory says loops add compute but no persistent memory, while appended tokens add memory ([Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30757)). A matched comparison of a 6-step loop against ft12 therefore asks directly whether CODI's six entries are limited by compute per entry or by the number of entries.

### The design makes every loop count equal CODI at initialization

The recipe follows the choices the retrofit literature converged on, adapted so that the fine-tune starts exactly at the released checkpoint. Layers 0–3 form a prelude that runs once and writes its KV. Layers 4–7 form the loop block, which runs k times. Layers 8–11 form a coda that runs once, followed by `ln_f` and CODI's projection to make the next latent. Iteration 1 is exactly CODI. Later iterations apply x_r = x_{r−1} + g ⊙ (B_r(x_{r−1} + W_inj·e) − x_{r−1}). Here the gate g is ReZero/InfiLoop-style damping, W_inj re-injects the input e as in Huginn and LOTUS, both start at zero, and B_r is the block plus a depth-aware LoRA (rank 16–64, B matrix zero-initialized) that is active only for r ≥ 2, as in TaH. The looped layers keep their iteration-1 keys and values, so each step still adds exactly one entry per layer. The coda's entries are computed from the deepened state, so depth reaches the cache and the next latent through layers 8–11. Every iteration reuses the same position id. **With g = 0 every k reproduces the published checkpoint**, so fine-tuning can move away from 206 only if the loop earns it, which avoids AlwaysThink's drop at the start. Two KV alternatives belong in the ablation. One keeps the final iteration's entries, which Ouro shows are nearly lossless at decode (78.85 against 78.92 with all caches ([Ouro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741))) but which are not an identity at initialization. The other is TaH's duo-causal cache, where each depth keeps its own entries; this is the variant where F gets a job.

Training samples k from {1, 2, 3, 4} with P(k=1) ≈ 0.25, following Kuo et al., where moderate randomness (±5) beat both no randomness and ±10 ([Kuo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29983)). It backpropagates through every iteration for k ≤ 4, and evaluation runs k = 1 through 8. The objective must carry a per-step signal, because answer-only fine-tuning already found no use for steps 7–12 and Huginn's failures show that a free loop gets ignored. The SIM-CoT checkpoint ships its step decoder, which supplies that signal without CODI's teacher pass; on CODI, use CODI's distillation loss instead. GPT-2 is pre-LN, so each pass adds to the residual norm, and the coda's input norm should be tracked against k. Huginn needed sandwich normalization, and McLeish et al. switched to Muon after AdamW diverged, but at our scale the zero-initialized damped gate is the cheaper safeguard. Under the pinned transformers 4.46.2, `GPT2Attention` returns a new `present` without mutating `layer_past`, so keeping iteration 1's entries means keeping one returned tuple and discarding the rest. That is about 60 lines, in a new script rather than in the study's `scripts/latent_gate/`. The hook points are the per-step loop in [`codi.py`](../../dsa/bench/models/codi.py) and the explicit position ids in [`stage1_eval.py`](../../scripts/latent_gate/stage1_eval.py). [`fetch_simcot.py`](../../scripts/latent_gate/fetch_simcot.py) currently discards the `decoder.*` tensors and needs a variant that keeps them.

### One loop arm costs about the same as ft12

Stage 1's own logs anchor the costs. At batch 32, bf16 and 1,024 updates, **ft6 took 436 s and ft12 683 s per 32,768 examples** ([ft6 metrics](../../results/latent_gate/stage1/codi/training/ft6/metrics.jsonl); [ft12 metrics](../../results/latent_gate/stage1/codi/training/ft12/metrics.jsonl)). That puts one extra 12-layer latent forward and backward pass at about 41 s. If wall-clock scales with layer evaluations per step, a 4-layer block at k=4 costs the same as ft12. The table applies that rule to answer-only cost; a per-step loss adds an unmeasured 30–60%.

| Arm | Layer evaluations per step | s per 32k examples | h per 385k-example epoch |
|---|---|---|---|
| ft6 (k=1) | 12 | 436 (measured) | 1.4 |
| Middle-4, k uniform on 1–4 | 18 (mean) | ≈560 | 1.8 |
| Middle-4, k=4 / whole-12, k=2 | 24 | ≈683 (= ft12) | 2.2 |
| ft12 | 24 | 683 (measured) | 2.2 |

The prices are third-party figures: an A100-80GB at about 7.52 Colab units per hour, with other sources reporting 10–15 ([McCormick](https://mccormickml.com/2024/04/23/colab-gpus-features-and-pricing/); [Thunder Compute](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/runpod-vs-google-colab-pro-gpu-comparison)). At those rates a four-arm pilot of about 100k examples per arm costs roughly 17–21 units, and a full epoch of one loop arm about 2–3 A100-hours. The Stage 1 logs do not record the GPU, so time 100 updates before budgeting.

## Decoding imports outside facts but throws away latent content

Hybrid latent and text reasoning is crowded, and the papers that gain are trained for the hybrid format. Token Assorted puts a latent prefix before text and scores 48.7 against 42.7 for CoT on Llama-3.2-1B ([Token Assorted](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03275)). SpiralThinker alternates latent blocks with generated text steps and reaches **56.56 on Llama-1B** ([SpiralThinker](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1605.pdf)). Its baselines look weak, though: it has no explicit-CoT row, its retrained CODI scores 51.02 against the 55.6 CODI's authors report for that model, and Pause tokens score above it at 53.37. HRPO mixes a sampled token into every latent input under RL and scores 72.0 against 71.1 for GRPO ([HRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18454)). Training-free mixing barely moves GSM8K. Soft Thinking changes it by +0.08 to +0.22 points ([Soft Thinking](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15778v1)), SwiReasoning gains +1.5 to +2.8 averaged over math and STEM ([SwiReasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05069v2)), and Chang et al.'s decode-time edits to 3–8B CODI and Coconut models gain +0.4 to +1.8, with +0.6 from soft re-embedding of decoded distributions ([Chang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01243v1)). The nearest decode-style result at GPT-2 scale is PCCoT's "w/ CoT decoding," which scores **50.42 against 49.48 on GSM8K but 39.80 against 49.23 on the natural-language variant** ([PCCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18582v2)).

The sharpest warning comes from CODI itself. GPT-2 models fine-tuned to emit the *decoded* intermediate results as their chain of thought score **34.0 (results only) and 35.7 (operators plus results), against 43.7 for the latents** ([CODI](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)). Coconut reads continuous thoughts as holding several candidate frontier nodes at once, and hard decoding collapses that ([Coconut](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)). D therefore trades latent content for discrete commitments. It can win only where the committed tokens carry something the latents lack, which on GSM8K means correct arithmetic.

D's most useful asset is already on disk. The `internlm/SIM_COT-GPT2-CODI` checkpoint contains SIM-CoT's auxiliary step decoder, a fully trained 163.0M-parameter GPT-2 copy in BF16 ([checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/internlm/SIM_COT-GPT2-CODI)). It conditions only on the projected latent and is trained to emit the matching `<<a op b=c>>` step. Two limits matter. Latents past a problem's gold step count got no decoder loss, and steps beyond the seventh slot were merged into the last one ([SIM-CoT model.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/InternLM/SIM-CoT/main/CODI/src/model.py)). The paper also reports no per-step decoding accuracy and says "the auxiliary decoder is removed at inference" ([SIM-CoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)). The decoder's reliability is therefore an open measurement, and a small result in its own right. CODI GPT-2 has no such decoder, and the logit lens recovers only the first token of a number.

The calculator is where D could genuinely add accuracy, and also its main confound. GSM8K's `<<48/2=24>>` annotations were introduced for calculator use ([GSM8K](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)), Calc-X trains explicit-CoT models to call one ([Calc-X](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15017)), and HybridCoT keeps math operations as text inside a latent scheme ([HybridCoT](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/126670)). No paper combining latent CoT with a calculator was found, so that slice is open. But gpt2-sft already writes `<<…>>` steps and gets the same calculator for free. A latent-plus-calculator result must beat **gpt2-sft plus the same calculator** at equal or fewer forward passes, or the contribution collapses to "use a calculator." Injected tokens count as forward passes: decoding at 24 latents and injecting three steps of about 8 tokens each is roughly 48 passes, against about 25 generated tokens for explicit CoT.

D carries three further risks. A wrong decode hardens into a confident wrong token. The student never saw tokens between `<bot>` and `<eot>`, so injected text is a distribution shift. And each injected step adds about 8 positions, while RoPE answer distance accounts for all of CODI-1B's depth loss ([memory](/Users/stevotrujillo/.claude/projects/-Users-stevotrujillo-Desktop-DSALT/memory/heldout-scale-results.md)), so D could help GPT-2 and still hurt LLaMA. Two of D's variants overlap with the analysis study rather than adding accuracy: choosing among depths by which decoded chain verifies, and stopping when the decode repeats. Both are training-free readout methods, and their ceiling is the 9–15 extra answers that a union over depths already measures.

## Recursive attention fixes counting, not content

### Per-entry gates cannot cancel duplicates; a key-key log-partition can

"Attention that shapes attention" is a large family, but most of it targets other problems. Talking-Heads, DCMHA, Multi-Token Attention and Nexus aim at expressivity. Differential Transformer and gated attention aim at noise and sinks. FoX and stick-breaking add recency, and NSA and Quest add efficiency. Only a few members compare keys with keys, which is what duplicates require. ToMe adds log s to merged tokens because that "performs the same operation as if you'd have s copies of the key," and removing the term costs an off-the-shelf ViT-L 1.4 points ([ToMe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09461)). R-KV scores redundancy by key cosine and is lossless at 10% of the cache on reasoning traces ([R-KV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24133)). LUCID places an exponentiated key-key kernel inside attention as a value preconditioner and gains 18% on BABILong ([LUCID](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10410)). Selective Attention is parameter-free and lets tokens mask earlier tokens, with constraints that keep it from ever boosting attention ([Selective Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02703)). None of these has been applied to latent-thought entries. Multi-Token Attention, the most expressive option, used 5.3B tokens even for continued training and runs at **5.7K against 54.3K tokens/s** unoptimized ([MTA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00927)), which rules it out on this budget.

The useful theory is short. **A per-entry score cannot cancel duplicates.** Any bias computed from one entry alone treats identical copies identically, so k+1 copies still act like one entry with logit z + log(k+1), and cancelling that requires knowing k. The impossibility covers an MLP gate, SSMax, head mixing and query-aware top-k selection. It also fits two failures from Stages 0 and 1. CODI's learned gate could tell copies apart only by non-content signals such as cycle phase, and it learned phase-parity kept sets. Eviction by attention score kept the attractor copies and scored **77, the worst policy** ([memory](/Users/stevotrujillo/.claude/projects/-Users-stevotrujillo-Desktop-DSALT/memory/dsa-pivot-latent-eviction.md)). The gate's features have not been probed, so this account is plausible rather than proven.

The minimal correct fix is a key-key bias I will call KK-LSE: b_j = −α·log Σ_l exp(β(k̂_j·k̂_l − 1)) over the latent entries, per head. It is minus the log-partition of a key-to-key attention row, so one attention pass literally produces the bias for another. It cancels exact copies for every k, stays within [−log n, 0] so it never boosts, needs 288 scalars (α and β per head), and fits inside the existing `_attn` patch ([gate.py](../../scripts/latent_gate/gate.py)). The query-conditioned version, the "recursive attention" proper, builds its kernel from the query's own logit differences and from value similarity. That matches the near-copy bound already checked in this project, which caps the output error at tanh(ε/4)·‖v_ref − mean of the other values‖ + η regardless of how many copies there are ([memory](/Users/stevotrujillo/.claude/projects/-Users-stevotrujillo-Desktop-DSALT/memory/codex-review-takeaways.md)).

### Readout reweighting caps at the 6-step score

F changes only how the readout weighs entries that already exist. Past step 6, CODI and SIM-CoT entries mostly repeat earlier content, so de-duplicated attention over 24 entries sees roughly what attention over 6 sees. The project's evidence says exactly that: `keep_first` at 24 scores 205 against 206 at 6, no budgeted policy or gate exceeds 6 steps, and the union over depths adds only 9–15 answers. Published gains from redundancy-aware selection, such as R-KV at 105% of full-cache accuracy and TRIM-KV at **75.8 against 65.5** on AIME24 ([TRIM-KV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03324)), come from pruning model-generated distractors in *explicit* traces. Removing information-free copies of useful entries is a different problem.

The route to a stand-alone gain would be to train with KK-LSE switched on, so that 12–16 steps stop collapsing. Our own data argue against it. CODI ft12 already holds depth without any dedup (210 at 12, 203 at 24) and still does not beat ft6. Dedup *hurt* CODI ft12 (182 against 210), though not SIM-CoT ft12 (206 against 207). PCCoT's 12-beats-6 result came from training from scratch, not from an attention change. Making repetition free also removes the pressure to make later steps distinct, which risks entrenching the collapse, and SIM-CoT itself blames large-count collapse on "insufficient step-level supervision" ([SIM-CoT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20317)), not on attention.

F's training-free form therefore belongs to the analysis study. There it would tighten the hold result through a five-rung ladder that changes one thing at a time: `dedup_soft`, then per-head hard key cosine, a soft key kernel, a key-and-value kernel, and finally the query-conditioned kernel. It also carries a testable prediction, that a kernel in key space should not hurt CODI ft12 the way embedding-space dedup did. The impossibility argument would give that paper a clean explanation of the CODI gate's failure.

### The pairing with A works only if the loop remembers its iterations

The user proposed F together with a looped transformer. That pairing deserves a precise answer, because it depends on the KV policy. In the recommended KV-neutral loop, each latent still writes one entry per layer, so at 6 steps **there is nothing for F to de-duplicate**. In TaH's duo-causal design, each depth keeps its own cache and deeper iterations attend to shallower ones ([TaH](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08577)). Every latent then writes k entries per looped layer, and as the iterations converge those entries become near-duplicates. That is the same dilution the project measured, and exactly what KK-LSE cancels. The published evidence on attention over iteration states cuts both ways. Without a correction it collapses: InfiLoop's no-decay version scores **38.4%/40.2% against 82.1%/89.7%** with learned decay on Sudoku-Extreme ([InfiLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11570)). With a correction the results are mixed: Dreamer's depth attention gains 3.8 points at depth 32 but loses 2.3 at depth 16 ([Dreamer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21582)).

In Zhang's terms, a duo-causal loop adds memory as well as compute, and KK-LSE keeps that memory from diluting the readout. **A duo-causal loop with KK-LSE is therefore the fair version of the user's idea.** It is cheap, since with at most 24 entries per head the key-key kernel costs almost nothing. It is also the hedge if the KV-neutral loop fails: before declaring A dead, test whether deeper iterations needed to see the shallower ones.

## A staged plan puts a kill point before each spend

### Stage 0 runs on evaluation only

Six cheap checks come first. Together they take about 1–2 T4-hours, or 2–3 compute units at 1.19 units per hour ([McCormick](https://mccormickml.com/2024/04/23/colab-gpus-features-and-pricing/)).

| Check | What to run | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Run the loop wrapper at k=1, and at any k with g=0, on the released CODI and SIM-CoT checkpoints | It must reproduce 500 of 500 answers, as every earlier control did, before any training |
| Overwrite probe (A, training-free) | Rerun the full 12-layer step r ∈ {2, 3, 4} times at the same position id, keeping only the last iteration's entries; add a damped version and an ETD-style angular-distance knee on latent positions | Records accuracy (I expect about 200–206), the union over r against the 9–15 from depth, and decoder step accuracy against r; confirms the 4–7 block. ### Stage 1 is a four-arm pilot of about 20 compute units

Start from SIM-CoT GPT-2. Its released decoder supplies a per-step loss without CODI's teacher pass, its trail settles without CODI's odd-even cycle, and it gave the best ft6 score (215). Train four arms with the same objective (answer cross-entropy plus the step-decoder loss), the same 98,304 examples in the same order, the same learning rate and the same seed. The arms are ft6; ft12, matched in layer evaluations to a 4-layer loop at k=4; the middle-4 loop with k uniform on 1–4; and a parameter-matched k=1 arm that adds the same LoRA at iteration 1 without looping, to rule out "more parameters." Keep Stage 1's base learning rate of 2e-5, which did not wreck the checkpoint. Give the zero-initialized loop parameters their own higher rate, as Stage 1 already did with 1e-3 for its gate ([ft6 args](../../results/latent_gate/stage1/codi/training/ft6/args.json)); for loop parameters that rate is untested. Evaluate on validation at a preregistered k=3 plus the k=1–8 sweep, and report the 4+ step subset separately (173 of 500 questions, where ft6 gets about 30 right), because that is where extra serial work per entry should show.

### Stage 2 buys seeds, the F pairing and the 1B port only after a pass

Confirmation re-runs the passing arms at three seeds and evaluates on the 1,319-question test set, about 7–8.5 A100-hours at pilot size. It then replicates on CODI GPT-2 with CODI's distillation as the per-step signal, adds the duo-causal loop with and without KK-LSE, and runs one full epoch for the winning arm and ft12. Port to LLaMA-3.2-1B only after GPT-2 passes. The loop wrapper only re-calls decoder layers, and McLeish et al.'s (4, 6, 4) layout on Llama-3.2-1B is a template. Because Part C's full fine-tune hurt SIM-CoT-1B, the 1B arm should **freeze the base weights and train only the loop parameters**, so that k=1 stays exactly the base model and any change belongs to the loop. A is the only candidate whose extra compute adds no RoPE positions, and at 1B adding positions is what costs accuracy.

The paired k-sweep inside one model carries most of the statistical weight. The weights are identical and only the loop count changes, so seed noise cancels, and that is what makes a single-seed pilot decision possible when two fine-tunes differ by ±10. The +3-point final bar follows the spreads that other papers report on the same task: a seed standard deviation of 1–1.6 points (PCCoT, LOTUS) and a binomial standard error of about 1.35 points on 1,319 questions ([PCCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18582v2); [LOTUS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31779)).

### Both outcomes are publishable

If A passes, the paper reports the first loop inside sequential latent steps that adds no KV entries and beats more appended steps at matched compute. It comes with a dose-response, ideally with decoder-measured step accuracy rising with k, a direct contrast with "Trading Depth for Time," and a built-in reason to transfer to RoPE models. If A fails, the paper is a matched 2×2 null: 6 or 12 steps, crossed with k = 1 or 3, under the same per-step objective. It would carry the first per-step reliability numbers for SIM-CoT's released decoder and an error breakdown that locates GPT-2's ceiling in readout, arithmetic or planning. That fits a workshop paper or a section of the analysis preprint, and it would show that PCCoT's 12-beats-6 is a from-scratch recipe effect that a converged 6-step checkpoint cannot be fine-tuned into. Either way, the KK-LSE impossibility result goes to the analysis paper.

## Conclusion

The three ideas target three different bottlenecks: compute per latent step (A), outside knowledge (D), and how the readout counts entries (F). They are not three routes to the same fix. The project's own record already rules out the third as a source of new accuracy. The second is worth what a calculator is worth to gpt2-sft, and that has to be measured, not assumed. Compute per step is the only untested bottleneck left that could lift a CODI-style model above 206. Testing it also settles a theory question the analysis paper raises but cannot answer: whether six latent entries are short of memory, which more steps would supply, or of computation, which an inner loop supplies.

The user's instinct to pair recursive attention with a looped transformer is sound, but the order runs the other way. In a KV-neutral loop F has nothing to protect. F becomes necessary exactly when the loop is allowed to remember its iterations, which makes it a dependency of the richer A variant rather than a fix for today's chain. The 1B finding tilts the whole choice toward A, because only A spends its extra compute without adding RoPE positions. SIM-CoT's released decoder will be most useful as the instrument that shows whether a deeper latent step computes a better intermediate result. As a way to inject text back into the chain, it is weakest.
