# Learned latent eviction holds a narrow niche

**Status, 12 November 2026.** This review was written on 2 November, before Stage 0 finished, and its plan columns and
recommendations describe the plan at that time. Since then the decode-label check failed, so H3 and the label arm were
dropped; Stage 1's H2 failed on both GPT-2 models; and soft dedup, which this review proposed, held the 6-step score on
both without training ([Stage 1 results](LATENT_GATE_STAGE1_RESULTS.md)). The held-out and scale study then tested the
diagnosis on untouched data and at 1B ([held-out results](LATENT_GATE_HELDOUT_RESULTS.md)).

No published paper combines the four parts of reframed Dynamic State Anchoring: a learned gate over the KV entries of appended continuous thoughts, hard eviction to a budget when the loop runs past its trained depth, gate labels from logit-lens decodes matched to gold intermediate results, and the diagnosis that readout attention dilution causes the drift. Every part has a close neighbour, though, and the gate mechanism itself is prior art. The closest competitors are **TRIM-KV**, a learned retention score added as a log-bias to attention logits, with a capacity hinge and argmin eviction, which beats the full cache on math reasoning; **KaVa**, which combines R-KV eviction with per-latent KV supervision in a CODI-family student; **AGCLR**, which puts learned gates inside a Coconut loop on GPT-2 GSM8K; and **InfiLoop and RecurTrace**, which rescue depth extrapolation in state-replacing loops by decaying or windowing access to past loop states. What remains open is narrower than the gate and easier to defend. It has three parts. First, the measurement that CODI and SIM-CoT lose answers past their trained 6 steps. Second, the exact-KV-copy probe showing that a settled latent still degrades the readout, which is a counterexample to the dynamical "settling implies depth-safety" guarantee. Third, verifiable decoded labels as supervision for a retention gate. Reviewers will want six rival explanations ruled out: positional shift, sink-mass reallocation, latent-state drift, coupling to the training iteration count, train/test eviction mismatch, and readouts that bypass the latents. They will also want the gate to beat cheap fixes the current plan omits, chief among them multiplicative log-n sharpening, a log-count dedup correction, and training under random eviction with no learned gate. The literature's sharpest warning concerns the depth-gain claim (H2). Every published treatment that stabilizes a loop past its trained depth preserved peak accuracy at best, so H3, labels against answer loss alone, is where the method's novelty is tested, and H2 is where its impact is.

## No method combines all four parts, but each has a close neighbour

Five literature sweeps on 2 November 2026 covered latent chain-of-thought, looped and recurrent-depth transformers, attention dilution, KV eviction and learned gating, and targeted novelty checks. None found a paper with even two of the four parts in the latent-CoT setting. The neighbours cluster by the part they cover, and a reviewer will cite each one against the matching claim. The table ranks them by how hard each one presses on novelty.

Read together, the table narrows what the paper can claim. Five things are **not novel**:

1. The soft log-gate mechanism (FoX, Selective Attention, TRIM-KV).
2. Soft training followed by hard eviction (DMS, Dynamic Context Pruning).
3. Latents decoding to intermediate results. CODI's own Table 3 reports that the top-5 decoded values match the reference results 97.1%, 83.9% and 75.0% of the time for 1-, 2- and 3-step problems ([CODI](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)).
4. The odd-even alternation of CODI latents ([PMPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09928)).
5. The general argument that attention dilution justifies eviction. A 2026 long-context paper states that "irrelevant tokens can dilute attention away from useful evidence" and gives theory that retaining useful tokens reduces dilution ([Make Each Token Count](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09649)).

Four things **appear open**:

1. Measuring CODI and SIM-CoT past their trained depth. The only past-depth number found for any latent-token reasoner is LOTUS going from 70.0 to 69.3 with one extra iteration ([LOTUS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31779)).
2. The copy probe, which establishes readout reweighting in append-only latent CoT.
3. Verifiable decoded labels as supervision for a retention gate.
4. Any gain from extra depth, if H2 holds.

The safe framing pitches the diagnosis as a contribution in its own right. The gate is then "additive logit gating, as in FoX, Selective Attention and TRIM-KV, applied to latent entries and supervised with checkable labels."

## Readout reweighting explains drift where dynamical theories cannot

The attention theory is settled, and it predicts the project's measurement in general form:

- **Softmax must disperse.** If logits are bounded, every softmax weight falls as Θ(1/n) as keys are added. In a transformer over a finite vocabulary, this "must happen everywhere" at large enough sizes ([Veličković et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01104)).
- **Attention fading.** As n grows, the largest softmax output shrinks toward zero. The proposed fix multiplies the logits by s·log n ([SSMax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19399)).
- **log n is the critical scale.** A 2026 ICLR paper proves that β_n ≍ log n is the critical scaling. Weaker scaling collapses attention to uniform; stronger scaling turns it into the identity ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05554)).

The project's duplication identity is the same algebra. Under softmax, k+1 identical keys with logit z contribute (k+1)e^z = e^{z+log(k+1)}, so duplicating an entry k times adds log(k+1) to its logit ([LATENT_GATE_IDEA](../docs/LATENT_GATE_IDEA.md)). Sigmoid attention needs an additive −log n bias to stay stable at any length, which is the mirror-image correction ([Ramapuram et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04431)). None of this work targets latent thought tokens. In the latent and filler-token literature, the only attention-overload hypothesis is a one-line, unmeasured speculation in the pause-token paper that beyond an optimal pause count "the self-attention mechanism becomes overwhelmed" ([Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02226)).

The latent-CoT papers report degradation with more latents, but always as a **training-count** effect, and they explain it differently:

- CODI's accuracy peaks at 6 training latents, and the authors blame "optimization challenges" from the longer sequence ([CODI](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)).
- PMPS finds that CODI "drops sharply at 8" ([PMPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09928)).
- SIM-CoT traces Coconut's collapse at 5 latents to homogeneous latents left without step-level supervision ([SIM-CoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)).
- Coconut sees a "slight performance drop accompanied by increased variance" at three thoughts per step ([Coconut](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)).

None runs a trained checkpoint past its count at inference. The project's numbers are therefore new to the literature: CODI gets 206, 192 and 173 of 500 correct at 6, 12 and 24 steps, and SIM-CoT gets 204, 185 and 170 ([LATENT_GATE_IDEA](../docs/LATENT_GATE_IDEA.md)).

The looped-transformer literature has a different explanation, and it is the main rival. Bansal et al. named the failure "overthinking". They fixed it with input recall and a progressive loss that stops the net from counting iterations, which extended prefix sums from 32-bit training strings to 512-bit strings at over 97% ([Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826)). Think Shallow, Solve Deep sorts operators into settling, marginal and drifting regimes using Lyapunov exponents. Its Proposition 1a guarantees that the decoded answer stops changing once the remaining state displacement falls below the decoder's margin ([Think Shallow](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18222)). Blayney et al. bound the change in attention by the change in the residual stream, so stable states imply stable attention ([Blayney et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.11791)). Both results quietly assume the readout sees the current state in a *fixed* context.

Append-only latent CoT breaks that assumption. A fixed point appended N times draws softmax mass N·e^s / (N·e^s + Σ_j e^{s_j}), which tends to 1 as N grows, so the readout drifts while the state does not. SIM-CoT is the natural counterexample, and the literature lacks one:

- Its latents settle: a step-24 latent still identifies its question among 500 in 97.6% of cases, against 67.4% for CODI.
- It still loses as many answers as CODI.
- Exact copies of the step-6 entry reproduce its whole drop: 172 against 170 at 24 entries ([LATENT_GATE_IDEA](../docs/LATENT_GATE_IDEA.md)).

Other findings bridge the dynamics and attention accounts in the same way. CODI behaves as a "stable attractor" ([Duraipandian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09698)), SIM-CoT's trailing latents repeat the final answer ([SIM-CoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)), and repeating a token provably makes final representations "arbitrarily close" as length grows ([Barbero et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04267)). Each of these turns convergence into near-duplicate keys, and the softmax identity turns near-duplicate keys into a log-count boost. No paper found draws that link.

The split between state-replacing and append-only recurrence also shows up in which bounded caches work. Ouro decodes with only the last recurrent step's KV at 78.85 on GSM8K against 78.92 with all four caches; using only the first step's KV collapses it to 18.73 ([Ouro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)). CODI shows the opposite pattern. Feeding only the last six latents loses badly, scoring 165 before step 12 and 143 before step 24, both below the plain long trail that keeps the early latents ([CODI_TRAIL](../docs/CODI_TRAIL.md)). The latent-CoT literature agrees that early entries carry the content. Early latents are causal hubs ([Chang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01243)), and in CODI Llama-1B, patching latents 3 and 5 hurts accuracy while patching the last two does not ([Cywiński et al., LessWrong, not peer-reviewed](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YGAimivLxycZcqRFR/can-we-interpret-latent-reasoning-using-current-mechanistic)). So the recency defaults that work in looped models (Huginn's ring buffer, Ouro's last-step cache) should fail in CODI, and that asymmetry is itself reportable.

Two cautions limit the claim. First, the general idea that bounding what later iterations see rescues depth is already published: InfiLoop's no-decay ablation halves accuracy, and RecurTrace prefers a 3-loop window to a 5-loop one. Only the append-only, main-cache, copy-probe version is new. Second, the dilution account is complete for SIM-CoT but **covers only about half of CODI's drop**: copies give 188 against plain CODI's 173. CODI's question-specific content also decays, with retrieval of its own question falling from 98.8% at step 8 to 92.2% at 12 and 67.4% at 24 ([CODI_TRAIL](../docs/CODI_TRAIL.md)). For CODI, the dynamical rival explains half the loss, and a gate can address that half only by evicting drifted entries, not by repairing them.

A simple calculation points the same way. If latents 7–24 got the same logits as latents 1–6 and the latent block kept constant total mass, the share on latents 1–6 would fall exactly 4×. CODI's measured fall from 0.158 to 0.033 is 4.8×, and SIM-CoT's from 0.099 to 0.036 is 2.75×. So mass is also moving between the latent block and the question or sink, and that has to be measured, not assumed.

## The gate mechanism is prior art; its labels are new and fragile

The proposed soft gate, log g_j added to every logit that points at latent j with a penalty pushing Σg toward the budget, matches published designs closely:

- **FoX** adds D_ij = Σ log f_l, a data-dependent non-positive bias; ALiBi is the data-independent special case ([FoX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02130)).
- **TRIM-KV** adds (t−i)·log β_i and penalizes retention above the budget with a capacity hinge ([TRIM-KV](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03324)).
- **DMS** applies an additive log(1−α) mask with a Gumbel-sigmoid and a one-sided loss toward a target compression ratio, and reaches 8× compression in about 1,000 steps ([DMS](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05345)).
- **Dynamic Context Pruning** fine-tuned GPT-2 with drop scores that enter attention as log I, annealing the sigmoid into a step function for inference ([DCP](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15805)).
- **SAS** injects selector scores into the logits "in log form" so that the language-modeling loss trains the selector ([SAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13141)).

The Stage 1 arm `gate12`, a penalty of 0.1 × max(0, Σg − 6), is in effect TRIM-KV's capacity hinge and DMC's one-sided compression loss ([Stage 1 plan](../results/latent_gate/stage1/PLAN.md)).

These papers also carry four implementation lessons:

1. **A sum penalty does not make gates binary.** DMC sharpens its gates with a Gumbel-sigmoid at τ = 0.1 ([DMC](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.09636)), and DCP anneals its sigmoid. Both directly shrink the soft-versus-hard gap the plan measures as `gate` against `gate_soft`.
2. **log g needs a floor.** NAtS reports that −∞ masks give invalid gradients ([NAtS](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13251)).
3. **Eviction should be delayed.** DMS finds that immediate eviction "drastically deteriorates scores for every tested sliding window length", and LazyEviction finds that more than 95% of reasoning tokens regain attention after a gap ([LazyEviction](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15969)).
4. **Gates should only reduce attention and never touch the sink.** Selective Attention never masks BOS or a token's own position ([Selective Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02703)). The plan already protects the question and `<bot>`.

The label source is the gate's distinctive asset. In the learned-eviction literature, gate targets are attention-derived, distributional or outcome-based:

- Locret regresses to the maximum attention any answer token pays ([Locret](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01805)).
- ForesightKV distils a "golden eviction" computed from future attention, then refines it with GRPO ([ForesightKV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03203)).
- ThinKV ranks thought types by counterfactual KL ([ThinKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01290)).
- KaVa scores the teacher's cache by answer attention and key redundancy.

On the latent side, labels shape the latents or rank rollouts, never a gate. SIM-CoT decodes each latent into its GSM8K-Aug step text with an auxiliary decoder. A Coconut process reward model trained on Monte-Carlo labels reaches only 54.09% F1 and lifts best-of-16 accuracy from 31.08% to 33.36% ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12167v2)).

Two results argue that a verifiable label beats an attention-derived one. "Dormant" tokens, which get near-zero attention but are needed later, defeat attention-based eviction outright: six standard methods score 0% at a 16-entry budget ([Transactional Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11288)). And selectors distilled from dense attention rank entries out of line with their effect on predictions ([SAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13141)). Intermediate results act like RaaS's "milestone tokens", which are read, used and then fade ([RaaS](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11147)). That is the pattern a decode label can capture and an attention score can miss.

Label validity is the weakest link. Three sources support it: CODI's Table 3 decode matches, Cywiński et al.'s patching evidence, and Liang and Pan's "faithful bridge states" on short-horizon tasks ([Liang and Pan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00449)). Three threats cut against it:

- **No one-to-one mapping.** PMPS finds that logit-lens readouts "do not establish a one-to-one mapping to explicit CoT steps" ([PMPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09928)).
- **Per-entry labels have the wrong granularity.** SCIT, run on CODI-GPT2 itself, finds that counterfactual arithmetic transfers "primarily through value-cache suffix trajectories" in layers 8–9. A one-entry suffix reaches 0.203 target win against 0.984–1.000 for suffixes of three or more entries, and newly trained seeds "fail to reproduce" the carrier ([SCIT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27265)). If the computation lives in multi-entry value trajectories, a per-entry, top-1, residual-stream label measures the wrong unit.
- **Decodability has been over-read.** A 2026 paper argues that decodable intermediate values have been over-read as evidence of a compressed scratchpad ([Observable Patterns](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12689)).

There is also a design-specific risk. CODI separates each intermediate result with "a seemingly meaningless continuous token" ([CODI](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)). Those operator-phase latents never decode to a number, so every one of them gets label 0. The `gate12_label` binary cross-entropy at weight 1 will push all their gates toward eviction before anyone has checked that they are expendable. Gate features based on geometry alone would not rescue this: Coconut's correct and incorrect thoughts are geometrically indistinguishable, with IsoScore* near 0.013 for both ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12167v2)).

A diagnosis of looped models' halting gates adds a design warning. Simple post-hoc confidence readouts "often match or outperform" learned gates, and the failure comes from the trajectory that joint training induces, not from limits of the gate ([Popescu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20519)). The project already found the same ordering: summed token log-probability beat depth agreement as a confidence signal ([CODI_TRAIL](../docs/CODI_TRAIL.md)). This argues for fitting the gate on frozen latents before any joint fine-tuning.

The literature's priors on H2 are pessimistic but not closed:

- **Anchoring restores stability, not gains.** Think Shallow, Solve Deep repaired Huginn with "latent anchoring" toward a shallow state, restoring digit-copy from 0.00 to 1.00 at 128 recurrences. Yet "no treated model exceeds the base's peak competence at any depth" ([Think Shallow](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18222)).
- **Bounded windows also plateau below the peak.** RecurTrace holds 47.5% at 16 loops, below its 54.7% two-loop peak ([RecurTrace](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03379)).
- **The gate leaves CODI's training bottleneck in place.** CODI attributes its training-count peak to gradients that must travel 6+ steps to the first latent. A cache budget does not shorten that backpropagation path, because every latent still feeds the next as an input embedding. Huginn's backpropagation through only the last 8 iterations ([Huginn](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05171)) and Bansal's detached progressive restarts target exactly that bottleneck.
- **More latents can help when trained for.** PCCoT accuracy "stably improved with the increase of the number of latent thought tokens" up to 24 ([PCCoT](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.47.pdf)), and Huginn reaches 47.23% on GSM8K at 64 recurrences. Huginn trains with a heavy-tailed recurrence distribution of mean 32, so 64 sits in its training tail, not beyond it.
- **A hard cap limits memory.** Theory separates appended tokens, which add persistent memory, from compressed loops, which cannot ([Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30757)). A hard budget of 6 caps persistent memory at 6 entries, and with CODI's odd-even alternation only about three of them carry numbers. That is an unforced handicap on exactly the 4+ step problems H2 targets.

## Six rival explanations each need a named control

The literature supplies at least six alternatives to "the readout's attention spreads over more latent entries." The project's measurements already weaken some of them. Each needs a control that a reviewer can read off a table.

| Rival explanation | Literature behind it | What the project's data already show | Decisive control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positional shift**: answer tokens sit at absolute positions unseen in training | Length hurts even when every distractor is masked: at least 7.9% loss with 30K masked tokens ([Du et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05381)); temperature fixes NoPE but not RoPE ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12224)) | Reading out at the 6-step position leaves accuracy unchanged, 193 vs 192 and 174 vs 173 ([CODI_TRAIL](../docs/CODI_TRAIL.md)); Stage 0's `keep_first` isolates position | Confirm the copy probe's position ids, then rerun copies with the readout at the 6-step position |
| **Sink and question mass reallocation** rather than dilution within the latents | Sinks act as key biases ([Gu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10781)); an output gate cuts first-token attention from 46.7% to 4.8% and improves length extension ([Gated Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06708)) | Only the share on latents 1–6 was measured; the 4.8× vs 2.75× falls imply mass moves outside the block | Split readout mass into BOS/sink, question, latents 1–6 and extra latents at 6, 12 and 24 steps; add a learned null-logit baseline ([gpt-oss](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10925)) |
| **Latent-state drift** (dynamics) | Settling/drifting regimes and fixed-point fixes ([Think Shallow](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18222), [STARS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26733)) | Explains about half of CODI's drop and none of SIM-CoT's | The copy probe (done); a terminal fixed-point arm |
| **Iteration-count coupling** from fixed-depth training | Randomized loop counts cut the run-to-run standard deviation of OOD addition accuracy from 9.9 to 5.9 and raise it from 22.1 to 30.9 ([Kuo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29983)); Ouro peaks at its trained T=4, AIME24 64.70 → 39.00 at T=8 ([Ouro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)) | Not yet tested | `ftrand` (planned); a progressive-loss arm |
| **Train/test consistency**: a model trained under eviction wins for that reason, not because it selects well | Untrained models under eviction generate longer, with 38–58% of samples hitting 32K ([AbridgeKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20641)); eviction loss is "an information gap" ([KV-Rescue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15797)) | `window12` is the only trained mask without a learned gate | Train at 12 steps with *random* eviction to 6 and no learned gate |
| **Readout bypasses the latents** | A Coconut answer's top-10 attended tokens all come from the question ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22441)); Coconut latents act as placeholders ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21711)) | Own trail 206 vs mean trail 63 on GSM8K, but 400 vs 355 on SVAMP ([CODI_TRAIL](../docs/CODI_TRAIL.md)) | Report a trail-swap check per held-out set; weight evidence to sets where the trail matters |

The pause-token result separates two versions of the dilution story. Pause tokens share one learned embedding, so extra pauses are close to pure added count, yet models fine-tuned with 10 pauses "degrade gracefully" across 5 to 25 pauses at inference ([Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02226)). Count alone therefore does not predict sharp loss. The extra keys have to be near-copies of keys the readout already weights heavily, which is what the copy probe built and what a settling latent produces. A cheap discriminating control is to append pause-like fixed embeddings, or latents from another question, instead of self-copies. If the readout-dilution story is right, copies of a high-logit entry should hurt more than equally many low-logit entries.

The same logic exposes a gap in the planned temperature rival. The Stage 1 `logn` policy adds −log(n/6) to every latent entry once n > 6 ([Stage 1 plan](../results/latent_gate/stage1/PLAN.md)). That restores the latent block's total mass relative to the question but leaves the within-block share of latents 1–6 as diluted as before. It tests the block-mass hypothesis, not sharpening. SSMax-style scaling instead multiplies the logits, keeping attention focused only when the maximum logit beats the rest by about 1/s ([SSMax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19399)). If the extra latents are near-copies of trained ones, they share logits, and multiplicative sharpening cannot separate them, while a per-entry gate can. That contrast is the cleanest argument that the gate is needed, but only if both multiplicative and additive variants are run. The duplication identity also yields a training-free baseline the plan lacks, which this report proposes; no source tests it. Cluster latent keys by similarity and add −log(cluster size) to each member. This exactly cancels exact copies and approximates the correction for near-copies.

Three additions matter most because they are cheap and could collapse a claim before any training spend.

1. **Multiplicative SSMax and soft dedup belong in Stage 0.** If either matches the label oracle at fixed weights, the gate's fixed-weight case reduces to a one-line correction, and its value must come entirely from H2 and H3.
2. **The random-eviction trained arm and a compute-matched `ft6` belong in Stage 1.** Without them, a positive H2 cannot separate gate selectivity from the known benefit of training under eviction or from twice the compute per update.
3. **Label validation should run before H3, not after it passes.** SCIT's value-suffix result and the zero-label operator phase both predict that the labels could be internally consistent yet causally wrong, and H3 cannot detect that on its own.

Two inexpensive diagnostics would strengthen the write-up whatever the outcome. The first checks whether learned gates on exact copies converge to 1/(k+1), the analytic optimum. The second reports gate values separately for the number and operator phases.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what the paper's main contribution should be. Its most defensible result is not the gate but the finding that append-only latent reasoning breaks the depth-safety guarantee proved for looped models. A settled state is not a safe state when every step appends a key, because the guarantees in Think Shallow, Solve Deep and Blayney et al. assume a fixed context. That result survives a failed H2, so the stop rule's diagnosis write-up has its own audience. The gate enters a crowded field of mechanisms. Its only distinctive asset is the verifiable label, and the label is also its weakest link, since CODI-GPT2's computation appears to ride on multi-entry value trajectories and its operator-phase latents always get label 0. Sep-Latent's full text, and a forward-citation check of SIM-CoT and PMPS, are the remaining reads that could move the novelty verdict. Several 2026 neighbours (Sep-Latent, Dynamic K-Head, Make Each Token Count, STARS, Popescu et al.) were read only at abstract level.

The work should be ordered by risk. Test label validity and the two analytic fixes, soft dedup and multiplicative log-n, before any training run. Then treat H2 with a prior set by the literature: stabilization past trained depth has so far preserved peak accuracy without exceeding it, in Huginn, RecurTrace, LOTUS, PMPS and SIM-CoT. A positive H2 would be the first such result for latent CoT, and it would stand only with the random-eviction, compute-matched, fixed-point and budget-sweep arms beside it. The contrast between Ouro's lossless last-step cache and CODI's collapse under a recency window also suggests what any successful policy must do: keep early entries and evict redundant ones. Dedup and decode labels encode that rule, and the recency defaults borrowed from looped models get it backwards.
