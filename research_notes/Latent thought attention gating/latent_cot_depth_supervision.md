# Latent CoT: behaviour vs number of latent steps, decoding of latents, step-level supervision, adaptive latent length

Notes compiled 2026-11-02. How much of each source I read is marked in brackets:

[abs] = abstract or arXiv landing page only; [snippet] = search-result snippet or third-party summary only, so verify before citing;
[prior] = read in this project's earlier sessions (2026-11-01/02 notes) and not re-fetched today.

## Q1. How many latent steps each method uses, what happens when the latent count is scaled in training and at inference past training, and whether anyone reports (and explains) degradation past the trained depth

### Takeaway
Nearly every latent-CoT paper uses a fixed small latent budget (6 for Coconut/CODI/SIM-CoT/PMPS on GPT-2 GSM8K-Aug; 8 for PMPS Llama-1B; 12-24 for PCCoT/KaVa). Several report that *training* with more latents hurts (CODI past 6, PMPS's CODI at 8, Coconut collapsing at 5 in SIM-CoT, PCCoT/KaVa with too many Jacobi iterations). Nobody found reports *inference* past the trained latent count for CODI or SIM-CoT. The nearest results are LOTUS (R=6 run at 7, 70.0 to 69.3), Pause tokens (inference pause count shifted), and recurrent-depth/looped-transformer work, where drift past training depth is known and has competing explanations (drifting dynamics, spurious loop-length correlation). The explanations given are "optimization challenges" and "latent homogeneity" for training-count effects, and dynamical drift for recurrent depth. Readout-attention dilution over a growing latent KV cache appears only as a one-line speculation in the Pause-token paper and as the generic "attention fading" result of Scalable-Softmax.

### Cited Findings
**Per-paper records**

- **Coconut.** Hao, Sukhbaatar, Su, Li, Hu, Weston, Tian. "Training Large Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space." arXiv:2412.06769 (Meta FAIR; venue not shown on arXiv page). [full] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)
  - GSM8K uses c=2 continuous thoughts per step over 3 stages, so 6 latent thoughts at evaluation. ProntoQA and ProsQA use c=1 with up to 6 stages. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)
  - Fig. 8(II) tests c ∈ {0,1,2} on GSM8K: accuracy rises with c. App. C.1: c=3 gives a "slight performance drop accompanied by increased variance", with a sharp loss spike when three thoughts are added at once. This is a training-count effect, explained as optimization instability. *Motivates.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)
  - On ProsQA, inference is forced to k ∈ {0..6} thoughts by placing `<eot>` manually; accuracy rises with k (Fig. 3). This is at or below the trained count, never above it. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)
  - GPT-2 GSM8K: CoT 42.9, Coconut 34.1±1.5, iCoT 30.0, Pause 16.4, No-CoT 16.5 (Table 1). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)
  - Per a later paper's related-work section, Coconut tried a binary stop classifier and found it no better than a constant latent length [snippet]. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21581v1)

- **CODI.** Shen, Yan, Zhang, Hu, Du, He. "CODI: Compressing Chain-of-Thought into Continuous Space via Self-Distillation." arXiv:2502.21074 (King's College London; arXiv v3, 23 Sep 2025; no venue on the page). [full] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)
  - Default is 6 continuous thoughts "matching the setup in Coconut". *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)
  - Fig. 5 varies the number of latents used in training: accuracy peaks at 6 for both CODI and Coconut. Beyond 6, "the increased sequence length introduces optimization challenges, outweighing any potential gains". Sec. 7 adds that gradients to the first latent travel 6+ steps. The paper does **not** test more latents at inference than in training. *Motivates* (degradation with more latents) and opens a gap (no inference-time test, and a different explanation from ours). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)
  - The exact per-count accuracies are only in Fig. 5, not in the text. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)

- **SIM-CoT.** Wei, Liu, Zang, Dong, Cao, Wang, Qiu, Lin. "SIM-CoT: Supervised Implicit Chain-of-Thought." arXiv:2509.20317 (Fudan / Shanghai AI Lab; v2, 25 Sep 2025; no venue on the page). [full] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)
  - Fig. 1(a): Coconut accuracy rises from 1 to 4 latents, then collapses at 5, reaching a worst point of 12.5%. They call this "latent instability" and attribute it to missing step-level supervision, which causes latent homogeneity. Table 4(b): inter-latent distance is 28.34 normally vs 4.21 in the failed case; distance to the vocabulary centre is 28.34 vs 39.39. Failed latents decode mostly to numbers and lose operators (Fig. 1(b,d)). This is a training-count effect. *Motivates* (an alternative explanation: homogeneity, not attention). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)
  - The ablation trains SIM-CoT with up to 8 latents. It reports no train/inference mismatch. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)

- **PMPS.** Li, Chen, Ju, Yao, Li, Lan, Zhu, Wang. "Structural Process Supervision for Latent Chain-of-Thought Reasoning." arXiv:2609.09928 (SJTU / Taobao; v2, 28 Sep 2026; 2026 preprint). [full] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09928)
  - Uses 6 latents for GPT-2 and Qwen2.5-0.5B and 8 for Llama-3.2-1B. In the latent-count ablation (App. E, Fig. 7a), PMPS beats CODI at every count except 2, PMPS peaks at 6-7, and "CODI drops sharply at 8". The values are in the figure only. No model is run past its trained count. *Motivates.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09928)

- **PCCoT.** Wu, Teng, Tu. "Parallel Continuous Chain-of-Thought with Jacobi Iteration." EMNLP 2025 main (arXiv id believed to be 2506.18582, not verified this session). [full via PDF text] — [Source](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.47.pdf)
  - Uses c=24 latent tokens with T=3 Jacobi iterations; their continuous-CoT baseline uses 12 latents and Pause uses 24. With T=0,1,3, accuracy "stably improved with the increase of the number of latent thought tokens c" (App. Fig. 3, c up to 24). *Context: more latent tokens can help when trained for them.* — [Source](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.47.pdf)
  - As T grows, accuracy improves up to about T=3, "then starts to decrease (except for c = 12 latent tokens)", and training becomes unstable at large T. They also report "the latent tokens do not converge in multiple iterations after training". *Motivates* (more latent iterations past the sweet spot hurt; non-settling latents). — [Source](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.47.pdf)
  - Table 1 numbers could not be cleanly attributed to model and column from the PDF text extraction. The legible fragments are a PCCoT cell of 53.35±0.18 and CODI cells of 49.7 and 55.6. Treat these as unverified. — [Source](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.47.pdf)

- **KaVa.** Kuzina, Pióro, Whatmough, Ehteshami Bejnordi. "KaVa: Latent Reasoning via Compressed KV-Cache Distillation." arXiv:2510.02312, ICLR 2026 poster. [full] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02312v1); [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008323)
  - Uses M=24 latents with 3 Jacobi iterations (the PCCoT setup). Fig. 6 shows accuracy drops beyond some iteration count for both 12 and 24 latents. No inference past the trained count. *Motivates.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02312v1)

- **Pause tokens.** Goyal, Ji, Rawat, Menon, Kumar, Nagarajan. "Think before you speak: Training Language Models With Pause Tokens." arXiv:2310.02226 (ICLR 2024 per common citation; the arXiv page shows v3, Apr 2024). [full, first 100k chars] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02226)
  - Fine-tuning uses M_ft ∈ {10, 50} and inference defaults to M_inf = M_ft. Changing M_inf is "a severe test-time distribution shift". PausePT_PauseFT is "robust to a wide test-time shift"; Fig. 8: performance "degrades gracefully" for M_inf ∈ [5, 25] when M_ft=10, except a drop on GSM8K with M_ft=50. Zero pauses "breaks its performance spectacularly". This is the clearest train/inference latent-count mismatch study, but the tokens are fixed embeddings, not recurrent latents. *Supports/motivates.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02226)
  - The authors speculate that beyond an optimal number of fine-tuning pauses, "the self-attention mechanism becomes overwhelmed". This is the only explicit attention-overload hypothesis found in the latent/filler-token literature, and it is speculation with no measurement. *Supports.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02226)

- **Filler tokens.** Pfau, Merrill, Bowman. "Let's Think Dot by Dot: Hidden Computation in Transformer Language Models." arXiv:2404.15758 (2024). [abs] — Filler tokens help on two hard algorithmic tasks, but learning to use them "requires specific, dense supervision to converge". The abstract says nothing about filler-count extrapolation. *Context* (dense supervision is needed for useful non-text tokens). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15758)

- **iCoT / stepwise internalization.** Deng, Choi, Shieber. "From Explicit CoT to Implicit CoT: Learning to Internalize CoT Step by Step." arXiv:2405.14838 (2024). [abs] — Removes CoT tokens gradually. GPT-2 Small reaches up to 99% on 9x9 multiplication; Mistral-7B gets over 50% on GSM8K with no intermediate tokens. It has no latent tokens at all, so there is no latent-count axis. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14838)

- **CCoT.** Cheng, Van Durme. "Compressed Chain of Thought: Efficient Reasoning Through Dense Representations." arXiv:2412.13171 (2024). [abs] — Uses variable-length continuous "contemplation tokens"; "the number of contemplation tokens can be adjusted" on demand. The abstract gives no ratio or extrapolation numbers. *Context/competes* (variable latent length by design). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13171)

- **CoLaR.** Tan, Li, Ju, Luo, Song, Luan. "Think Silently, Think Fast: Dynamic Latent Compression of LLM Reasoning Chains." arXiv:2505.16552 (v6, Feb 2026; no venue on the page). [abs] — Trains with "a compression factor randomly sampled from a predefined range", and inference picks the factor by prompting. Chains are 53.3% shorter than explicit CoT at a cost of 4.8% performance. The RL stage shortens latent chains by 82.8% and gains up to 5.4% on harder math. Randomized latent length in training is a training-time alternative to eviction. *Competes.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16552)

- **Token Assorted.** Su, Zhu, Xu, Jiao, Tian, Zheng. "Token Assorted: Mixing Latent and Text Tokens for Improved Language Model Reasoning." arXiv:2502.03275 (v2, Sep 2025). [abs] — VQ-VAE discrete latent tokens replace the initial reasoning steps, and latent and text tokens are mixed at random during training. The abstract does not state the latent count. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03275)

- **SoftCoT++.** Xu, Guo, Zeng, Miao. "SoftCoT++: Test-Time Scaling with Soft Chain-of-Thought Reasoning." arXiv:2505.11484 (2025). [abs] — Scales test-time compute by diversifying soft thoughts ("multiple specialized initial tokens" plus contrastive learning), not by adding more latent steps. The soft-thought count was not visible. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11484)

- **LightThinker.** Zhang, Zhu, Sun, Luo, Qiao, Du, Zheng, Chen, Zhang. "LightThinker: Thinking Step-by-Step Compression." arXiv:2502.15589, EMNLP 2025 (oral). [abs] — Trains the model to compress thoughts into gist tokens and "discards the original reasoning chains" using specialized attention masks. It adds a "Dependency" metric. The abstract does not say outright that KV entries are evicted. *Competes* (learned compress-then-discard of reasoning context; explicit CoT). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15589)

- **LOTUS.** Fan et al. arXiv:2606.31779 (2026 preprint; this repo's upstream). [prior] — Table 6: an R=6 model run at R=7 goes from 70.0 to 69.3, "running more than at training time brings no further gain". This is the closest prior past-depth measurement, one step only and not CODI. *Motivates / partial novelty overlap on "past-depth measured".* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31779)

- **Learning When to Stop.** Ning, Kuo (Yen-Ling), Gomes. "Learning When to Stop: Adaptive Latent Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning." arXiv:2511.21581 (Nov 2025). [full] — Uses CODI-style SFT on Llama-3.2-1B, GSM8K-Aug. "Latent-6" is a fixed 6 steps; "Latent-6-by-1" allows up to 10. Results: Latent-6 49.73%, Latent-6+RL 50.11% with 52.94% fewer reasoning tokens; Latent-6-by-1 51.40%, +RL 48.14%. It does **not** test past the trained count. *Context/competes on adaptive length* (see Q4). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21581v1)

- **Huginn (recurrent depth).** Geiping, McLeish, Jain, Kirchenbauer, Singh, Bartoldson, Kailkhura, Bhatele, Goldstein. "Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach." arXiv:2502.05171 (2025). [full, first 100k chars] — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05171v2)
  - Training draws a random recurrence count r from a log-normal Poisson distribution with mean r̄=32 and backpropagates through the last k=8 steps. Test-time gains continue with more recurrences, but saturation is task-dependent. GSM8K uses the extra compute: 47.23% flexible at r=64 for the EMA model. Random depth during training is precisely the regime CODI lacks. *Competes* (a training-time fix). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05171v2)

- **Think Shallow, Solve Deep.** Viakhirev, Borodin, Almutairi, Barannikov, Abramov, Mkrtchian. "Think Shallow, Solve Deep: Controlling Recurrent Dynamics for Reliable Test-Time Depth." arXiv:2608.18222 (Aug 2026; submitted to AAAI-27; 2026 preprint). [abs] — Extra iterations can "improve, preserve, or degrade" answers depending on whether the operator settles, is marginal, or drifts. They give a sufficient depth-safety condition: per-step displacement small relative to the decoder margin. A terminal fixed-point objective gives depth-safe extrapolation (Sudoku 0.19 to 0.34 past the training horizon). Huginn-3.5B is "drifting". It does not mention attention, KV or latent CoT. *Competes* (the dynamical explanation). Our SIM-CoT evidence, which settles yet still degrades, is a counterexample to the claim that settling suffices when each step appends a KV entry. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18222)

- **Kuo et al.** Hsun-Yu Kuo, Chayti, Reizinger, Brendel, Jaggi. "Stabilizing Extrapolation in Looped Transformers via Learned Stochastic Stopping." arXiv:2606.29983, NeurIPS 2026. [abs] — Out-of-distribution variance comes from a spurious "sequence length and number of loops" correlation. "Introducing stochasticity into the number of loops during training sharply reduces OOD variance and stabilizes predictions across inference-time loop counts." RL-Halting "generally improves the accuracy-stability trade-off". Tasks are algorithmic only (binary addition, Dyck-1, Unique Set, Copy). *Competes* (the main training-time rival). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29983)

- **Scalable-Softmax.** Nakanishi. "Scalable-Softmax Is Superior for Attention." arXiv:2501.19399 (Jan 2025). [abs] — As input size grows, the largest softmax output goes to zero, which flattens attention ("attention fading"). SSMax fixes this, improves long-context retrieval, and can be swapped into already-pretrained models. This is the generic version of the hypothesised mechanism and a no-eviction rival fix (a log-n-type scaling; the exact formula was not visible in the abstract). *Supports the mechanism; competes on the fix.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19399)

- **Latent Recurrent Thoughts (LRT).** Chen, Fu. "Latent Recurrent Thoughts: Recurrent Refinement of Proposed Latents for Reasoning with Frozen LLMs." arXiv:2609.01117 (Sep 2026; 2026 preprint). [full, first 100k chars] — Trains with K=32 latents but keeps only K_infer=4 at test, a deliberate "train/inference asymmetry" copied from EBM-CoT, whose ablation "peaks near four". A search summary claimed that training with 4 collapses one benchmark to 9.5; that claim was **not** confirmed in the fetched text (App. E body not read). *Context* (fewer at inference than trained can be fine). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01117v1)

- **Cywiński, Bussmann, Conmy, Engels, Nanda, Rajamanoharan.** "Can we interpret latent reasoning using current mechanistic interpretability tools?" LessWrong/Alignment Forum, 22 Dec 2025 (not peer-reviewed). [full] — On CODI Llama-3.2-1B with 6 latent iterations, accuracy peaks at 5 latent iterations and the 6th does not improve it. *Context* (in-training saturation). — [Source](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YGAimivLxycZcqRFR/can-we-interpret-latent-reasoning-using-current-mechanistic)

- **Liang and Pan.** "Do Latent-CoT Models Think Step-by-Step? A Mechanistic Study on Sequential Reasoning Tasks." arXiv:2602.00449 (Jan 2026, v2 Sep 2026; 2026 preprint). [abs] — On deeper sequential tasks, CODI "does not reliably maintain a full latent rollout". It either compresses into a late-intermediate pathway or loses the latent signature. Predictions come from "late fusion at the answer readout". *Motivates* (long chains fail; consistent with our 5+-step drop). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00449)

### Inferences
- Every "more latents hurts" result found concerns models *trained* with more latents (CODI Fig. 5, PMPS Fig. 7a, SIM-CoT Fig. 1a, Coconut App. C.1, PCCoT/KaVa iteration sweeps). The explanations offered are optimization difficulty, latent homogeneity, and training instability, not readout attention dilution. The proposed "inference past trained depth → attention spread" diagnosis appears untested in the latent-CoT literature found.
- The Pause-token paper is the closest train/test count-mismatch study, and its own speculation ("self-attention overwhelmed") matches the hypothesis. It is worth citing as a precursor, but it measured nothing about attention.
- Two rival explanations must be ruled out explicitly: (a) dynamical drift (Think Shallow, Solve Deep; Kuo et al.), and (b) generic softmax attention fading with n (SSMax). Our SIM-CoT result (settled trail, cos about .95, yet the same drop; exact KV copies reproduce it) directly separates (a) from attention reweighting. (b) is the *same* mechanism in general form, so the novelty has to be the latent-CoT measurement plus the fix, not the mechanism.
- Training-time rivals that make the readout robust to latent count without eviction: random latent/loop counts (Huginn, Kuo et al., CoLaR's random compression factor) and SSMax-style scaling. A paper proposing a gate needs these as baselines.

### Gaps
- The exact per-count accuracies in CODI Fig. 5, PMPS Fig. 7a, SIM-CoT Fig. 1a and the PCCoT/KaVa iteration figures are only in the figures; the text extraction did not give them.
- Original SoftCoT (arXiv:2502.12134) latent count and Token Assorted's latent count were not verified (abstract only).
- Huginn's GSM8K behaviour beyond r=64 was not visible in the portion read.
- No paper was found that runs CODI or SIM-CoT checkpoints past 6 latents at inference. This was not exhaustively searched (about 20 searches/fetches today plus earlier sessions).

## Q2. Which papers decode or probe latent thoughts (logit lens, attention analysis) and find intermediate results?

### Takeaway
Decoding CODI/Coconut latents to intermediate results is well established: CODI's own Sec. 5.1, Coconut Fig. 9, SIM-CoT's decoder, Cywiński et al. on CODI Llama-1B (latents 3 and 5 hold step-1 and step-2 results, causally via patching), and Liang and Pan (logit lens, probes and patching on CODI). The odd/even (number vs transitional) alternation was already noted qualitatively by CODI ("separated by a seemingly meaningless continuous token"), by a LessWrong shortform, and quantitatively in similarity terms by PMPS. Counter-evidence exists for Coconut (Zhang et al. 2512.21711: latents act as placeholders). So "latents decode to gold intermediate results" is **not novel**. Using that decode as a *training label for an eviction gate* was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **CODI Sec. 5.1 / Fig. 6 / Table 3** [full]: the last hidden state is projected through the word embeddings (logit-lens style), and Fig. 6 shows the top-5 tokens per latent. Table 3: for correctly answered problems, the top-5 decoded intermediate results match the reference CoT 97.1% (1-step), 83.9% (2-step) and 75.0% (3-step). "Each intermediate result is separated by a seemingly meaningless continuous token" (placeholder/transitional states). In the attention example, z2 attends to "1" and "7" to produce "7", and the operator is not visibly attended. App. D: models fine-tuned on the decoded patterns reach only 34.1-35.7% vs CODI 43.7%. *Threatens novelty of "latents decode to intermediate results" and of the alternation observation; supports label validity.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)
- **Coconut Fig. 9** [full]: on GSM8K the first continuous thought often decodes to plausible intermediate variables. On ProsQA, latent "values" of frontier nodes behave like BFS (e.g., 0.33 for "lempus", then 0.87 for "rorpus"). *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)
- **Cywiński et al. (LessWrong, Dec 2025)** [full]: on CODI Llama-3.2-1B, the logit lens shows the step-1 intermediate value in latent 3 and the step-2 value in latent 5, with high top-1 probability across the dataset, including on wrong answers. The final answer is absent because the traces omit the last step. Patching latents 3 and 5 with means from prompts with *different* intermediate values "notably reduces accuracy"; same-value means leave it unchanged. Randomly patching the last two latents does not reduce accuracy. Steering latent 5 moves the answer as expected. *Threatens novelty of the decode finding; supports causal validity of decode labels at number-phase latents; supports the idea that later latents are expendable.* — [Source](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YGAimivLxycZcqRFR/can-we-interpret-latent-reasoning-using-current-mechanistic)
- **neverix shortform (LessWrong)** [snippet]: reproduces CODI attention maps with a dashboard and says that in Llama-3.2-1B "tokens that have an interpretable prediction under the logit lens and tokens that don't ... will predictably alternate". It cites Liang et al. 2026 on other arithmetic tasks. *Threatens novelty of the alternation observation.* — [Source](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/45RD3ysCSikrmM2mD/neverix-s-shortform)
- **Liang and Pan, arXiv:2602.00449** (2026 preprint) [abs]: logit-lens decoding, linear probes, attention analysis and activation patching on CODI with "known intermediate states" (polynomial iteration). It finds faithful bridge states on short tasks, "late fusion at the answer readout", and a lost rollout on deep tasks. *Threatens novelty of the probing methodology; supports the readout-centric view.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00449)
- **SIM-CoT** [full]: the auxiliary decoder decodes each latent into a step (Fig. 4: 0.3×120=36, 120−36=84, 3/4×84=63, 84−63=21). App. K: trailing latents repeat the final answer and are omitted from visualisations. No attention analysis. *Supports* (later latents are redundant copies, consistent with our KV-copy result). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)
- **PMPS, arXiv:2609.09928** (2026 preprint) [full]: cosine heatmaps on 100 GSM8K-Aug test items with GPT-2. CODI shows "odd-even collapse": within-odd 0.693, within-even 0.800, cross 0.250. SIM-CoT shows "tail collapse": tail 0.805 vs head 0.591. Logit-lens readouts "do not establish a one-to-one mapping to explicit CoT steps". No attention analysis. *Threatens novelty of the odd/even finding; partly contradicts label cleanliness.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09928)
- **Zhang et al., "Do Latent Tokens Think? A Causal and Adversarial Analysis of Chain-of-Continuous-Thought," arXiv:2512.21711** (Dec 2025) [snippet]: argues that Coconut latents are placeholders, with "minimal sensitivity to steering". Perturbation success is reported below 5% for Coconut vs up to 50% for CoT tokens (alphaXiv summary). Coconut exploits dataset shortcuts on MMLU/HotpotQA. *Challenges* (latents may not carry the reasoning; this concerns Coconut, not CODI). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21711)
- **Wang, Vu, Shareghi, Haffari, "Towards Inference-time Scaling for Continuous Space Reasoning," arXiv:2510.12167** (Oct 2025) [full]: Coconut GPT-2 thoughts show "geometric homogeneity": IsoScore* about 0.013 for both correct and incorrect thoughts, and t-SNE mixes them. Correct and incorrect latents are hard to tell apart geometrically. *Challenges* gate features that use only latent geometry. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12167v2)
- **Li et al., arXiv:2602.08783** (2026 preprint) [prior]: do-interventions on Coconut and CODI at a fixed T=6; mean and step-specific-mean single-step interventions on CODI Llama-3.2-1B. Its "two modes" are competing answer modes, not latent alternation. It never runs past T=6. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08783v3)
- **Chang et al., arXiv:2606.01243** (2026 preprint) [prior]: early latents are causal hubs. Training-free decode-time edits give +0.8 to +1.5 points for CODI on 3B-8B models, with no GPT-2. A whole-sequence transplant flips 2.5% of answers. K=6 fixed. It argues that latents are "steerable, semantic reasoning blueprints" (the counterpoint to 2512.21711). *Supports* (early latents matter most) and *competes* (decode-time editing). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01243)
- **Duraipandian et al., arXiv:2607.09698** (ICML 2026 FoGen workshop, non-archival) [prior]: GPT-2 CODI at T=6 is a stable attractor with two lobes and "moves between these modes". No step alternation measured and no past-depth run. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09698)
- **Dilgren and Wiegreffe, arXiv:2604.04902** (2026 preprint) [prior]: latent tokens are often unnecessary on logical reasoning. *Challenges.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04902)

### Inferences
- Our numbers add little beyond Cywiński et al. and CODI Table 3 except a GPT-2 rate with a chance baseline: 36-56% of number-phase latents match a gold result vs about 4% chance. They should be presented as label-validity checks, not as findings.
- Cywiński et al.'s patching (latents 3 and 5 are causal; the last two are not) is the strongest prior evidence that a decode-label-driven gate could keep "result" latents and drop others without loss. It is also the best template for the planned "validate decode labels against causal drop-one effects" check.
- The homogeneity results (2510.12167 for Coconut, SIM-CoT's failure analysis) suggest a gate needs features beyond raw latent geometry: decode-based features, attention-based features, or position.

### Gaps
- None of these papers measure how the readout's attention over latent positions changes with latent count. The CODI attention analysis is per-latent, not readout-level, at a fixed count of 6. No source was found reporting the "<eot> attention on early latents falls about 4x" type of measurement.
- The full text of 2512.21711 and of Liang and Pan was not read; their attention results are unverified.

## Q3. Which papers supervise each latent step, with what labels, and does anyone use verifiable intermediate rewards?

### Takeaway
Step-level supervision of latents exists in four forms. (1) Decoding each latent to the gold step text with an auxiliary decoder (SIM-CoT, using GSM8K-Aug steps). (2) Soft prototype alignment to teacher CoT embeddings with no step labels (PMPS). (3) Matching teacher KV caches compressed by eviction, per latent and per layer (KaVa). (4) Process rewards: Monte-Carlo PRMs for Coconut (2510.12167), and LLM-judge per-depth rewards for Huginn (LSRL, decoding each latent depth to text). RL on latent reasoning (HRPO, Soft Tokens Hard Truths, Learning When to Stop, Dropout-GRPO, Switch-GRPO, Özeren and Aßenmacher) uses **outcome** rewards, sometimes with length terms. No paper was found that uses *verifiable* intermediate-result matches (logit-lens decode == gold `<<a op b = c>>` result) as a reward or label, and none uses such labels to train a gate over KV entries.

### Cited Findings
- **SIM-CoT** [full]: an auxiliary decoder, identical in architecture to the LLM, takes latent z_k as a prefix and generates the k-th GSM8K-Aug step text s_k (e.g., "<<12*3=36>>"). L_step is cross-entropy over step tokens, and the total is λ_step·L_step + λ_lm·L_ans. The decoder is dropped at inference. GPT-2: Coconut 36.6 to 44.8 and CODI 42.0 to 42.6. Llama-1B: Coconut 33.2 to 42.2 and CODI 52.7 to 56.1. CODI 3B 60.8 to 62.3; 8B 61.1 to 64.1. *Threatens novelty of "intermediate-step labels from GSM8K-Aug" (same label source); competes.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)
- **PMPS** [full]: K learnable prototypes, Sinkhorn-Knopp soft assignment and a bidirectional cross-prediction loss between latent and teacher-CoT embeddings. It has "no direct token-level reconstruction targets". Total loss is L_base + λ_proto·L_proto. GPT-2 GSM8K-Aug: CODI 40.86, SIM-CoT 42.08, PMPS 44.35; Llama-1B: 53.90 / 56.03 / 56.56. *Competes* (process supervision without explicit labels). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09928)
- **KaVa (ICLR 2026)** [full]: the teacher's CoT KV cache is compressed by an R-KV-style eviction, with score S = λ·Importance + (1−λ)·Redundancy and λ=0.1. Importance is answer-token attention and redundancy is key cosine similarity. The top-M pairs are kept per head and layer, with M equal to the student's latent count (24). The student's latent keys and values are matched to them with an L1/MSE loss per layer and latent, on top of the CODI loss. Llama-1B: CODI 55.6 vs KaVa 56.5±0.4 (column inferred). *Closest competitor on "KV eviction + per-latent supervision"*: eviction is on the teacher side as a target-building device, not a gate on the student's latents at inference, and there is no depth extrapolation. *Competes / partial novelty threat.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02312v1)
- **Wang et al. 2510.12167** [full]: PRM and ORM heads on a Coconut GPT-2 backbone with 6 thoughts. PRM labels are Monte-Carlo labels from N=10 completions (MATH-Shepherd style; hard = any correct, soft = fraction correct). They are **not** verifiable intermediate-result labels. Deterministic 31.08%; Pass@16 oracle 42.61%; best PRM rerank at N=16 is 33.36%. PRM F1 is 54.09%. *Competes* (step-level latent reward), and it shows that rollout-based labels are weak. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12167v2)
- **LSRL** (Hangliang Ren et al., Findings of EMNLP 2025) [snippet]: a process-supervised GRPO variant for Huginn-3.5B. It decodes each recurrent depth into a partial solution and scores it with a GPT-4.1-nano grader. Reported gains are about +4.27% GSM8K and +2.06% MathQA (per RLTT's description, not checked against the paper). *Competes / threatens novelty of "decode each latent and score it"*: the grader is an LLM judge rather than a verifiable match, and the model is recurrent-depth rather than latent-CoT. — [Source](https://preview.aclanthology.org/author-page-yu-wang-polytechnic/2025.findings-emnlp.669.pdf); [RLTT description](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10520)
- **RLTT, arXiv:2602.10520** (2026 preprint) [snippet]: spreads reward across the full latent trajectory instead of only the last latent state. *Competes.* — [Source](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10520)
- **HRPO.** Yue, Jin, Zeng, Zhuang, Qin, Yoon, Shang, Han, Wang. "Hybrid Latent Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning." arXiv:2505.18454, NeurIPS 2025 [abs]: "integrates prior hidden states into sampled tokens with a learnable gating mechanism" and uses RL without CoT trajectories. The reward form was not visible in the abstract. Note the name collision: its "gate" mixes inputs and does not evict cache entries. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18454)
- **Soft Tokens, Hard Truths.** Butt, Kwiatkowski, Labiad, Kempe, Ollivier. arXiv:2509.19170 (Sep 2025) [abs]: RL for continuous CoT with soft tokens and input-embedding noise, scaling to "hundreds of tokens". It matches discrete CoT on pass@1 and beats it on pass@32. The best setup trains soft and runs inference hard. *Context.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19170)
- **Learning When to Stop, arXiv:2511.21581** [full]: the GRPO reward has only correctness, a format penalty and a relative length term (λ_penalty=1e-4, λ_reward=0.1). App. A's distillation variants ("Meaned Reasoning Loss", "Intermediate Block Loss") were unsuccessful. **No intermediate reward.** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21581v1)
- **Dropout-GRPO, arXiv:2606.10184** (Jun 2026) [snippet]: the latent phase is deterministic, so identical rollouts give zero group advantage. Dropout adds rollout diversity, moving Coconut on GSM8K from 27.29% to 29.01% pass@1. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10184)
- **Switch / Switch-GRPO, arXiv:2606.13106** (Jun 2026) [snippet]: adds boundary tokens so the GRPO ratio is defined at every text position. MATH-500 79.3%, +25.7 over the strongest Coconut-style baseline at the same scale. It describes CODI and Coconut as sharing "hidden-state recurrence". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13106)
- **Özeren and Aßenmacher, "Reinforcement Learning for Latent-Space Thinking in LLMs," arXiv:2512.11816** (SwissText 2026) [snippet]: GRPO applied directly gave no measurable gain, and the RL-trained latent models still lag language-space CoT on math. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11816)
- **Coconut-based PRM homogeneity** (2510.12167): the authors attribute weak discrimination to supervision that covers only text and answer, with no geometric constraint on latents. They suggest isotropy, diversity or contrastive biases. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12167v2)
- **Filler tokens** need "specific, dense supervision to converge". This is general support for dense intermediate labels. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15758)

### Inferences
- The label source (GSM8K-Aug `<<...>>` steps) is already used by SIM-CoT, which decodes the *entire step* with an extra decoder. The proposed contribution differs in two ways: (i) the labels are *verifiable matches of a logit-lens decode to gold results* (cheap, no extra decoder, binary), and (ii) they train a *retention gate* rather than shaping the latent itself. Any write-up needs SIM-CoT and KaVa as the explicit comparison.
- No work found uses verifiable intermediate-result matching as an RL reward for latent CoT. Per-step rewards come from MC rollouts (2510.12167) or LLM judges (LSRL). This looks like an open niche, with the caveat that LSRL is conceptually close.
- RL on deterministic latent rollouts is hard: zero group variance (Dropout-GRPO), and no gain from plain GRPO (Özeren and Aßenmacher). This argues for supervised gate training with labels over RL for a first study.

### Gaps
- LSRL's full text, exact label/grader protocol and GSM8K numbers were not verified; only an RLTT summary and the Anthology link were seen.
- The SIM-CoT λ weights and its code/checkpoint details were not visible in the fetched text.
- No search was done specifically for "latent PRM" papers on CODI. Some may exist that were not surfaced.

## Q4. Work that varies latent count adaptively (halting / adaptive latent length), and the closest competitors to the proposed gate

### Takeaway
Adaptive latent length is an active area: stop heads with RL on CODI-style Llama-1B (2511.21581), random compression factors (CoLaR), token-level adaptive halting in pretraining (2602.08220), and, for looped/recurrent models, KL-based exit (Huginn), learned halting gates diagnosed as weaker than post-hoc confidence readouts (Popescu et al. 2607.20519), and learned stochastic stopping (Kuo et al., NeurIPS 2026). All of these decide *when to stop*. None was found that decides *which past latent entries the readout may see*. Prior KV-eviction work on reasoning (LightThinker, Think Clearly, LazyEviction, R-KV via KaVa, Huginn's ring-buffer cache) targets explicit CoT or recurrent-depth caches, and none is trained with intermediate-result labels.

### Cited Findings
- **Learning When to Stop, arXiv:2511.21581** (Nov 2025) [full]: a linear binary head says continue or stop at each latent step, followed by SFT (steps = k+6, cap 10) and GRPO with a length term. 52.94% fewer latent tokens at 50.11% vs 49.73%. The head's supervised labels are not specified. *Competes on adaptive latent count.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21581v1)
- **Popescu, Sáez de Ocáriz Borde, Liò. "Adaptive Depth in Looped Transformers: Diagnosing Learned Halting Gates and Trajectory Readouts." arXiv:2607.20519** (Jul 2026; 2026 preprint) [abs]: a single exit distribution "entangles exit selection with trajectory formation". Fixed-prior depth supervision plus post-hoc confidence readouts "often match or outperform" learned linear/MLP halting gates. Gates fitted on frozen trajectories show that the failure comes from the trajectory joint training induces, not from gate expressivity. Ouro ponder gates are "competitive but not uniformly Pareto-optimal". Tested on modular arithmetic, parity and Ouro-1.4B/2.6B. *Challenges a learned gate*: a simple post-hoc readout may match it, and joint training can damage the trajectory. This is a required baseline and a design warning (consider training the gate on frozen latents). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20519)
- **Kuo et al., arXiv:2606.29983, NeurIPS 2026** [abs]: random loop counts in training stabilise predictions across inference-time loop counts. RL-Halting improves the accuracy-stability trade-off but "can also lock in a suboptimal computation". *Competes* (the main training-time rival to the gate). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29983)
- **Huginn, arXiv:2502.05171** [full, partial]: zero-shot adaptive exit when the KL between successive steps falls below 5×10⁻⁴ (MT-Bench 5.56 vs 5.63 standard). Also **zero-shot KV-cache sharing**: a fixed budget k per token, with iteration i writing slot i mod k. A budget of 4 scored 5.86 vs 5.63 standard on MT-Bench. *Competes / threatens novelty of a recency-eviction baseline over recurrent latent KV entries.* This is depth-recurrence, not CODI-style sequence latents, and the policy is a fixed recency rule, not a learned gate. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05171v2)
- **Zeng et al., "Pretraining with Token-Level Adaptive Latent Chain-of-Thought," arXiv:2602.08220** (Feb 2026; 2026 preprint) [abs + snippet]: a variable-length latent trajectory before each token, with "token-wise adaptive halting". A search snippet says it drops tokens whose reach probability falls below a threshold and reuses the KV cache across latent steps (unverified). *Competes on adaptive latent length.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08220)
- **CoLaR, arXiv:2505.16552** [abs]: a random compression factor in SFT gives a latent length controllable at inference, and RL shortens chains further. *Competes.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16552)
- **Think Shallow, Solve Deep, arXiv:2608.18222** [abs]: a terminal fixed-point objective makes extra depth "depth-safe". *Competes* (a training-objective rival). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18222)
- **FPRM, arXiv:2606.18206** [prior]: halts on hidden-state convergence. **Two-Scale Latent Dynamics, arXiv:2509.23314** [prior]: a second-order latent exit on FineWeb. Both handle stopping only. — [FPRM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18206); [Two-Scale](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23314v2)
- **Adaptive Latent Agentic Reasoning (ALAR), arXiv:2606.02871** [snippet]: switches per turn between latent and explicit CoT with a GRPO variant. **Latent Thought Flow** [snippet; arXiv id not confirmed]: a GFlowNet over variable-length continuous trajectories. — [ALAR](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.02871); [Latent Thought Flow summary](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/latent-thought-flow-efficient-latent)

#### Closest competitors to "a learned gate over latent-thought KV entries for depth extrapolation, trained with intermediate-result labels"
No single paper found combines (a) gating or evicting *latent-thought* KV entries, (b) to run *past trained latent depth*, (c) trained with *verifiable intermediate-result labels*. Ranked by overlap:
1. **KaVa (arXiv:2510.02312, ICLR 2026)**: KV-cache eviction (R-KV importance+redundancy) plus per-latent KV-level supervision of a CODI/PCCoT-style student. It overlaps on "eviction + per-step latent supervision"; it differs in that eviction compresses the *teacher's* explicit CoT cache into targets, with no inference gate and no depth extrapolation. **Threatens novelty partially; must be cited and contrasted.** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02312v1)
2. **SIM-CoT (arXiv:2509.20317)**: the same GSM8K-Aug intermediate-step labels per latent, the same CODI GPT-2 base. No gate, no past-depth test. **Competes on labels.** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20317)
3. **Huginn KV-cache sharing (arXiv:2502.05171)**: zero-shot fixed-budget ring-buffer eviction over recurrent latent KV entries, with no loss on MT-Bench. **Threatens novelty of the recency-budget idea; a required Stage-0 baseline.** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05171v2)
4. **LSRL (Findings EMNLP 2025)**: decodes each latent depth to text and rewards it per step (LLM grader). **Competes on "decode latents as supervision"** [snippet]. — [Source](https://preview.aclanthology.org/author-page-yu-wang-polytechnic/2025.findings-emnlp.669.pdf)
5. **LightThinker (arXiv:2502.15589, EMNLP 2025)**: learned compression and discarding of reasoning context with attention masks, on explicit CoT. **Competes on "learned eviction of reasoning entries".** — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15589)
6. **Think Clearly (Choi et al., arXiv:2507.08806, Findings EMNLP 2025)** [snippet]: training-free eviction of reasoning chunks scored by attention from an *end-of-thinking* token, on explicit CoT, with AIME/AMC gains. **Competes with the Stage-0 attention-score policy**; it is the closest analogue to the `<eot>`-attention measurement. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08806)
7. **LazyEviction (arXiv:2506.15969)** [snippet]: in reasoning, tokens regain attention after long gaps, so current or cumulative attention eviction discards them too early; it proposes lagged, windowed eviction. *Competes; also a warning for attention-score gates.* — [Source](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15969)
8. **Selective Attention (Leviathan, Kalman, Matias; arXiv:2410.02703, ICLR 2025)** [abs]: a parameter-free mechanism that reduces attention to unneeded context and allows buffer pruning (16x/25x/47x less attention memory at 512/1024/2048 for equal C4 perplexity). *Competes (generic learned masking).* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02703)
9. **Scalable-Softmax (arXiv:2501.19399)**: fixes attention fading without eviction. *Competes on the fix and supports the mechanism.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19399)
10. **Kuo et al. (arXiv:2606.29983, NeurIPS 2026)** and **CoLaR** (random latent length in training). *Training-time rivals for depth robustness.* — [Kuo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29983); [CoLaR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16552)
11. **AGCLR (arXiv:2606.07720)** [prior]: learned read/forget/write gates on a persistent memory for Coconut, with soft writes at every latent. No past-depth test, no CODI. GSM8K 34.0 vs Coconut 31.4 on GPT-2, single seed. *Competes ("learned gates on latent reasoning").* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07720)
12. **LOTUS (arXiv:2606.31779)** [prior]: the only past-trained-depth number found for a latent-token reasoner (R=6 to 7: 70.0 to 69.3). *Partial novelty overlap on the measurement.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31779)
13. **Popescu et al. (arXiv:2607.20519)**: post-hoc readouts often match learned gates. *Challenges the need for a learned gate; a required baseline.* — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20519)

### Inferences
- The defensible novel slice looks like this: (i) measuring that latent-CoT accuracy drops past trained depth for CODI and SIM-CoT, and that exact KV copies reproduce it (readout reweighting, not state degradation). Nothing equivalent was found; LOTUS has one step, and Think Shallow, Solve Deep attributes the drop to dynamics. (ii) A retention gate over *latent* KV entries trained on *verifiable* logit-lens-to-gold matches. Each component has a neighbour (KaVa for KV supervision, SIM-CoT for labels, Huginn/LightThinker/Think Clearly for eviction, Kuo/SSMax for depth robustness), but the combination was not found.
- Baselines a reviewer will ask for: Huginn-style recency ring buffer; attention-score eviction (Think Clearly / R-KV / H2O-style) with LazyEviction's caveat; SSMax-type log-n scaling of the readout; random latent counts in training (Kuo, Huginn, CoLaR); SIM-CoT and KaVa trained at 12 latents; a post-hoc confidence or decode readout without a learned gate (Popescu).
- Given Popescu et al.'s finding that joint gate training corrupts trajectories, train the gate on frozen latents first. Given Cywiński et al.'s patching, check that "result" latents (decode-matched) are the causal ones before using them as keep-labels.

### Gaps
- Classic KV-eviction methods (H2O, SnapKV, R-KV proper, Locret, TRIM-KV-style learned retention) and gated/forgetting attention (Forgetting Transformer, gated attention) were not researched here; that is presumably an adjacent researcher's scope.
- Whether any 2026 paper evicts or masks *latent* entries in CODI/Coconut specifically could not be confirmed either way. The targeted search ("prune evict latent thought tokens attention continuous chain-of-thought gate") returned nothing matching.
- The full texts of Popescu et al., Kuo et al., Zeng et al. 2602.08220 and Think Shallow, Solve Deep were not read (abstracts only), so their exact numbers and any attention-related analyses are unverified.
